# Findings

**H1 is falsified, and the kill criterion in `docs/03` is met.** Union sparsity at
k=4 / per-token 40% is **5.1%** against a **<20% → C1 dead** floor; at k=4 /
per-token 50% it is **10.8%** against a ≥30% gate. The result is not marginal: it is
95 SEM below the 50% gate and 155 SEM below the 40% gate, and every one of the 20
agentic traces lands in a 9.3–12.2% band.

## 1. The overlap premise does not hold on this model

C1 rests on the LLM-in-a-Flash observation that adjacent tokens activate similar
neurons. On Qwen3.8-27B at 50% per-token sparsity, adjacent-token mask Jaccard is
**0.380** — the active sets of consecutive tokens share barely a third of their
members. Union sparsity therefore collapses roughly geometrically in k
(29.1% → 17.4% → 10.8% for k = 2, 3, 4).

Quantitatively: if activity were **statistically independent** across adjacent
tokens, union sparsity would be `s^k` = 6.8% at k=4. The measured 10.8% is only
**1.59× better than independent**. Real adjacent-token structure exists, but it is
small — nowhere near the ~30–40%-of-per-token retention `docs/02` assumed
("per-token 50% sparsity is expected to degrade to ~30–40% skippable columns under a
k=4 union"; the measured value is 10.8%, i.e. **21% retention**, not 60–80%).

Extrapolating at the measured excess ratio, clearing the H1 gate would need ~66%
per-token sparsity — past the point where SpQt reports perplexity degradation becomes
noticeable. There is no sparsity setting at which C1 works.

## 2. Agentic code overlaps *less* than prose, not more

The thesis assumed the agentic-coding workload would be favourable. It is the
opposite. Controlling for realized per-token sparsity via the excess-over-independence
ratio, the WikiText control beats the agentic traces at every k and every target
(e.g. 1.79× vs 1.59× at k=4 / 50%). Raw Jaccard says the same: 0.437 prose vs 0.380
code. Code's token-level repetitiveness does not translate into activation-mask
reuse on adjacent tokens.

## 3. Where structure does exist, it is in the wrong 8% of the weights

Two site classes overlap substantially more than the rest:

| Site class | input is | Jaccard (50%) | k=4 union | share of tapped weights |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `attn-o` | softmax-attention output | 0.514 | 22.1% | 2.1% |
| `deltanet-out` | DeltaNet recurrent output | 0.502 | 20.0% | 6.2% |
| `attn-qkv`, `deltanet-in`, `ffn-up-gate` | residual-stream hidden state | 0.365–0.376 | 9.4–10.1% | 68.3% |
| `ffn-down` | SwiGLU activation | 0.376 | 11.2% | 23.4% |

The split is mechanistic: inputs that are *attention or recurrent outputs* are smooth
across adjacent positions, while the residual stream is not. Even so, neither
well-behaved class clears the gate on its own — `attn-o`'s 22.1% is roughly double the
model-wide figure but still under 30%, and 14.1% at the 40% target is under the 20%
kill floor. And the two of them are **8.3%** of tapped weights combined, so they
cannot move a byte-weighted number dominated by the FFN (70.3%) and the other
hidden-state-fed projections (68.3% including FFN-up-gate).

## 4. The DeltaNet question does not arise at Phase 0

`docs/02` C2 flagged Gated DeltaNet projections as the open research risk.
On the overlap axis they are unremarkable: `deltanet-in` (Jaccard 0.367) behaves
almost identically to `ffn-up-gate` (0.365) — unsurprising, since both read the same
post-layernorm hidden state. DeltaNet-specific *quality* risk (state drift) remains
untested and is now moot for C1. No depth band rescues the result either: across
8-layer bands spanning all 64 layers, k=4 union sparsity varies only between 9.4%
and 12.8%, drifting mildly downward toward mid-depth and back up at the output end.

## 5. What this caps the end-to-end speedup at

Decode here is bandwidth-bound, so a pass reading fraction `f` of the trunk runs at
`1/f`. MTP verification amortizes one pass over k draft tokens, so it can only exploit
the k-token *union*:

| | fraction read | ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| dense pass | 1.000 | 1.00× |
| sparse GEMV, batch 1 (the SpQt regime, unchanged by this result) | 0.491 | 2.04× |
| union-mask verification, k=2 | 0.709 | 1.41× |
| union-mask verification, k=3 | 0.826 | 1.21× |
| union-mask verification, k=4 | 0.892 | **1.12×** |

At k=4, adding union-mask sparsity to MTP is capped at **1.12×** against H3's ≥1.25×
gate — and that is a bandwidth upper bound that ignores index-collection overhead,
kernel inefficiency (SpQt reaches ~75–89% of ideal on GEMV), and the quality cost of
union masking. C1 cannot reach its target even with a perfect kernel.

**The one exception, stated so it is not mistaken for a rescue.** At k=2 the ceiling
is 1.41×, above the H3 gate. It is not a way to save the project: applying SpQt's
measured kernel efficiency drops it to ~1.1–1.25×, k=2 drafting yields a lower
`E[accepted]` than k=4 (giving back the speculation win that motivated the design),
and union masking's quality cost is unmeasured. The k=4 kill criterion in `docs/03`
is binding and is met; recording k=2 as a measured fact is not the same as clearing
the gate.

## 5b. Robustness: the fixed-threshold shortcut is not what killed H1

`docs/03` permits an empirical calibration quantile for Phase 0, and fixed thresholds
let realized per-token sparsity drift above target — tokens that happen to be less
sparse than the target contribute extra active columns and inflate every union they
join. That is the one plausible way the method could be *pessimistic*, so it was
tested directly: eight agentic traces were re-run with an **exact per-token, per-site
quantile** (`dump_masks.py measure --oracle`), which forces every token to realize
the target sparsity precisely. This is not implementable in a kernel — the threshold
would have to be derived from the activation it is thresholding — so it is a bound,
not a design.

| thresholds | per-token | k=4 union | if independent | excess |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fixed, calibrated (20 traces), target 50% | 50.9% | 10.8% | 6.8% | 1.59× |
| **per-token oracle** (8 traces), target 50% | 50.1% | **9.7%** | 6.3% | **1.54×** |
| fixed, calibrated (20 traces), target 40% | 40.8% | 5.1% | 2.8% | 1.83× |
| **per-token oracle** (8 traces), target 40% | 40.1% | **4.5%** | 2.6% | **1.74×** |

The oracle is **slightly worse**, not better. Its lower absolute union sparsity is
mostly just its lower realized per-token sparsity (fixed thresholds happened to land
~0.8 pp above target); controlling for that via the excess-over-independence ratio,
the two are within 3% of each other. Removing per-token threshold variance does not
recover overlap, because there is very little overlap to recover. H1 fails under the
strictest available masking rule, not merely under the calibration shortcut.

## 6. What survives

- **The Phase 0 method and tooling.** The instrumentation, the site decomposition, the
  calibration, and the aggregation are model-agnostic and reusable.
- **SpQt's batch-1 result is untouched.** Per-token sparsity is 50.9% at the 50%
  target with fixed calibrated thresholds — a 2.04× bandwidth ceiling for batch-1
  decode on this model. The negative result is specifically about *composing* that
  with multi-token verification.
- **A measured baseline for this model**: dense 4-bit decode on this machine runs at
  **17.9 tok/s** (M3 Max 36 GB, MLX 0.32.2), consistent with `docs/01`'s 14–18 tok/s
  estimate.

## 7. Not tested (and would need a new hypothesis, not a retry)

Magnitude thresholding was the only mask rule tried. Rotation-based (LaRoSA) or
rank-aware (R-Sparse) masks could in principle have different adjacent-token
structure, as could a predictor-based scheme (DejaVu). Nothing here measures them.
Any revival of C1 needs a rule whose masks overlap far more across adjacent tokens —
that is a different research question, not a tuning exercise on this one.

## Gate decision

**Phase 0 gate: FAIL — kill criterion met.** Per `docs/03` and the operating rules,
Phases 1–3 do not start. C1 is dead as specified; C2 and C3 are moot without it.
Awaiting review.

# Method

## What is measured

TEAL prunes the **input activation** of a weight matrix: columns of `W` whose
corresponding input element falls below a threshold are never read. So the unit of
measurement is not a matrix but a **site** — a distinct input vector. Matrices that
consume the same vector share one mask, and that is exactly the sharing a sparse
kernel exploits.

| Site class | in-dim | Consumers (Σ out-dim) | Layers | Share of tapped weights |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `attn-qkv` | 5120 | `q`(12288) + `k`(1024) + `v`(1024) = 14336 | 16 | 4.8% |
| `attn-o` | 6144 | `o`(5120) | 16 | 2.1% |
| `deltanet-in` | 5120 | `qkv`(10240) + `z`(6144) + `b`(48) + `a`(48) = 16480 | 48 | 16.6% |
| `deltanet-out` | 6144 | `out`(5120) | 48 | 6.2% |
| `ffn-up-gate` | 5120 | `gate`(17408) + `up`(17408) = 34816 | 64 | 46.9% |
| `ffn-down` | 17408 | `down`(5120) | 64 | 23.4% |

256 sites, **2,162,688 mask bits per token**, **24.35 G parameters** tapped. Excluded:
`lm_head` and `embed_tokens` (2.54 G — `docs/03` excludes the vocab head), and the
vision tower (`mlx_lm`'s `sanitize()` drops it; text-only serving per `docs/01`).

The shared-input assumption was verified empirically, not assumed: all 240 secondary
consumers were tapped alongside their primary over 6 decode steps and compared
element-wise — **240/240 exact matches, zero mismatches**, one call per matrix per
step.

## Thresholds

Fixed per site, TEAL-style, calibrated **offline on a held-out split** and then held
constant during measurement. `docs/03` permits an empirical quantile for Phase 0;
that is what was used:

> per-site empirical quantile of `|x|` over the calibration split, estimated from a
> 4096-bin `log2|x|` histogram (range `2^-24 … 2^12`, ≈0.6% value resolution) with
> log-linear in-bin interpolation.

This is deliberately **not** a per-token oracle quantile. With a fixed threshold the
realized per-token sparsity varies around the target — the honest setting, and the
one a kernel actually faces. Realized per-token sparsity is reported next to every
union figure so the reader can see how far the fixed thresholds drifted on the
measurement split.

Calibration split: 4,646 decode tokens — 6 agentic turns + 2 WikiText slices,
**disjoint from the measurement split** (drawn first and popped from the candidate
pool, so no assistant turn appears in both).

## Forward pass

Teacher-forced, decode mode, no sampling:

1. the trace prefix is prefilled in ≤2048-token chunks with the tap **off**;
2. target tokens are then fed **one at a time** (S=1) through the KV/SSM cache with
   the tap **on**.

Decode-mode statistics differ from chunked prefill (different DeltaNet state path,
different attention shapes), and MTP verification happens at decode time, so S=1 is
the correct regime. Logits are not computed — the trunk is called directly, skipping
`lm_head`.

Instrumentation is a **class-level monkeypatch** of `nn.Linear.__call__` /
`nn.QuantizedLinear.__call__` that identifies the site by object identity. No wrapper
modules, no changes to the parameter tree, no changes to numerics.

Masks are never stored. A rolling window of `k_max = 4` masks updates streaming
per-site accumulators; only summary statistics are written.

## Aggregation

- **union sparsity** for a k-token window = `1 − |∪ active sets| / D`. A column counts
  as skippable only if it is inactive for *all* k tokens.
- **adjacent Jaccard** = `|A∩B| / |A∪B|` on active sets of consecutive tokens.
- The **model-wide** figure is **byte-weighted**: skipping one input column of a site
  saves `Σ consumer output dims` weight elements, so sites are weighted by
  `in-dim × Σ out-dim`. This is the number that maps to bandwidth saved; the
  unweighted per-class numbers are reported separately.
- Windows never span a trace boundary.
- Per-trace accumulators run alongside the pooled ones, giving a between-trace SEM.
  The gate distinguishes "PASS with margin" (estimate ≥2 SEM beyond the threshold)
  from "within noise".

## Corpora and provenance

**Agentic traces (20).** Assembled by `analysis/build_traces.py` from the operator's
own local Claude Code session logs (`~/.claude/projects/**/*.jsonl`) — real
agentic-coding work, not synthesized. Each trace is one assistant turn that performs
file edits (`Edit`/`Write`), rendered with its tool calls expanded into the diffs and
file contents they carry, preceded by up to 2048 tokens of the conversation that led
to it. Only the assistant turn is measured; the preceding conversation and all tool
*output* are prefill, because MTP verification only ever operates on model-generated
tokens.

Selection is round-robin over (project × primary language) buckets from a shuffled
pool with a fixed seed (1337), deduplicated by content hash. Coverage:
5 projects, 10 languages (`ts`, `js`, `rust`, `python`, `c`, `shell`, `toml`, `json`,
`yaml`, `markdown`), 11,171 decode tokens.

This investigation's own session logs are excluded from the pool. Traces live in
`analysis/traces/` and are **gitignored** — they contain the operator's private source
code. Only aggregate statistics are committed.

**Control (4).** WikiText-2-raw-v1 `test`, 3,072 decode tokens, 256-token prefixes,
same pipeline. Present to separate "activation sparsity overlaps because adjacent
tokens are similar" from "…because code is repetitive".

## Reproducing

```bash
uv sync
uv run python analysis/build_traces.py                     # traces (gitignored)
uv run python analysis/dump_masks.py calibrate              # -> analysis/thresholds/
uv run python analysis/dump_masks.py measure                # -> analysis/summary/
uv run analysis/overlap.py --summary analysis/summary/phase0.json --report analysis/report.md
uv run analysis/overlap.py --check                          # exits 0
```

`overlap.py` reads only the committed summary statistics, so the tables above
regenerate deterministically without the model or the traces.

## Limitations (stated before the numbers, not after)

1. **Teacher forcing is a proxy for the draft stream.** MTP verification's k tokens
   are *draft* tokens. A rejected draft's activations are off-distribution relative
   to the accepted continuation measured here, so these figures are the
   high-acceptance limit. Phase 3 must re-measure union sparsity on the real draft
   stream; the gap is expected to widen union masks (lower union sparsity) as
   acceptance falls.
2. **Union masking changes outputs.** Per-token results include columns that token
   would have pruned. Phase 0 measures only *how many* columns survive the union, not
   what the quality cost is — that is Phase 1's job.
3. **Magnitude thresholding only.** No rotation (LaRoSA) or rank-aware (R-Sparse)
   variant was tried; a better mask rule could shift these numbers in either
   direction.
4. **Thresholds are per-site quantiles, not TEAL's block-wise greedy budget.** Phase 0
   asks whether overlap exists at a *given* per-token sparsity; allocating the budget
   unevenly across sites (Phase 1) will change the per-class mix, not the overlap
   phenomenon.
5. **One quantization artifact.** Everything is measured on
   `mlx-community/Qwen3.8-27B-4bit`. Activation distributions under llama.cpp's Q4_K
   will differ slightly.

## Deviations from `docs/03` (flagged per operating rules)

- **Six site classes instead of four.** `docs/03` names attn-QKVO / ffn-up-gate /
  ffn-down / deltanet-proj. Q/K/V and O do not share an input vector (5120 vs 6144),
  and neither do the DeltaNet in- and out-projections — lumping them would average
  over distinct distributions and hide which one a kernel can exploit. Both views are
  reported; the four-class table is included for direct comparison with `docs/03`.
- **One measurement run, not three.** `docs/03`'s "3 runs, report median" targets
  *timing* cells. Phase 0 produces deterministic statistics: two full re-runs of the
  same configuration were compared and are **byte-identical** (SHA-256 of the summary
  with `env`/`wall_seconds` stripped: `f92744a0fd132d0d…`). Three runs would report
  the same number three times. Phase 2/3 timing runs get the full 3-run median
  treatment.
- **`iogpu.wired_limit_mb` left at the default.** Raising it needs `sudo`; per the
  operating rules the default is recorded (`0`, i.e. system default ≈75% of 36 GB)
  and used as-is. Peak MLX memory was 15.6 GB, comfortably below that ceiling, so the
  limit is not binding for Phase 0.
